Automated and AI systems on farms in the Philippines: a briefing note for the Bureau of Animal Industry

Lexley Villasis, mentored by Henrike Gätjens

Briefing note in preparation · November 2026

Lexley Villasis investigated how farmed animals could be brought into Philippine policy on AI, and she looked for a first step that does not require a new law.

Poultry farms in the Philippines already use tunnel ventilation that is controlled by a central computer. Lexley found no documented case of AI being used on farmed animals in the Philippines so far, and no sign that failures of automated farm systems are recorded as a matter of animal welfare. Her argument to Philippine policymakers is therefore about preparing before AI arrives on farms.

Lexley's starting point is the Animal Welfare Act of the Philippines (Republic Act No. 8485, as amended by Republic Act No. 10631). According to the Act, its purpose is to protect and promote the welfare of all terrestrial, aquatic and marine animals in the Philippines by supervising and regulating the facilities in which animals are bred, kept, treated or trained. Section 3 of the Act requires the Director of the Bureau of Animal Industry to “supervise and regulate the establishment, operation and maintenance” of facilities in which animals are confined. The Bureau of Animal Industry belongs to the Department of Agriculture. In Lexley's reading, the Bureau can therefore ask farms how automated and AI systems are used, without any change to the law.

Lexley is writing a briefing note for the Bureau of Animal Industry. The briefing note asks the Bureau to collect evidence on how automated and AI systems are used in registered animal facilities. In Lexley's reading, the rules that implement the Animal Welfare Act already provide for research, surveys and an information system on animal welfare, and the Bureau could collect the evidence through them. The briefing note proposes questions for farms, such as which tasks are automated, whether a system makes or recommends decisions using AI, what alerts staff when a system fails, whether a manual backup exists, and whether a failure has ever harmed animals at the facility.

The briefing note is being finalised, and its legal references are being checked against the published texts. AIGAH will add the briefing note to this page, and later any response from the Bureau. Lexley continues the work with AIGAH after the incubator has ended.
