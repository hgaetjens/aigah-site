AI sandboxes and animal welfare law: a submission to the UK Animal Sentience Committee

Dr Laura Thomas-Walters, mentored by Henrike Gätjens

Submitted 7 October 2026

In May 2026 the UK government announced that its planned Regulating for Growth Bill will create powers for regulatory sandboxes. A regulatory sandbox is a supervised trial in which existing rules are relaxed for a limited time, so that new technologies such as AI can be tested under real conditions. If a trial is judged successful, the Bill is meant to allow the relaxed rules to be changed permanently.

Two government documents name protections that regulatory sandboxes are meant to leave in place. According to the briefing notes on the King's Speech, trials would operate under safeguards that include “protections for consumers, workers and human rights”. An earlier call for evidence on AI sandboxes, published by the Department for Science, Innovation and Technology, names consumer protections, safety provisions, fundamental rights, workers' protections and intellectual property rights as rules that the government intends to exclude from any modification. Animal welfare appears in neither list.

Neither document says which rules a regulatory sandbox may relax, and neither excludes the rules that protect animals. Many rules that protect animals are duties that a person has to carry out. Farmed animals whose welfare depends on frequent human attention must be inspected at least once a day, and a driver must stop and report after a collision that injures an animal such as a horse, a sheep or a dog. In the call for evidence, the government justifies AI sandboxes by saying that “some regulations needlessly assume activity will be undertaken by a person not AI”. A duty to inspect animals assumes a person, so the duty could be relaxed for a trial in which an AI system takes over the inspection. The published plans do not require anyone to assess what such a trial would mean for the animals.

Laura Thomas-Walters and AIGAH brought the missing protection for animals to the attention of the Animal Sentience Committee. The Animal Sentience Committee was established under the Animal Welfare (Sentience) Act 2022. It can report on any policy of the UK government, unless the matter is devolved, and a report examines whether the government has paid “all due regard” to the ways in which the policy might harm the welfare of animals as sentient beings. After each report, the Secretary of State must lay a response before Parliament within three months.

Laura asked the Committee how an outside organisation can propose a policy for review, received the Committee's stakeholder form and sent the completed form on 7 October 2026. The submission asks the Committee to write a short report now, while the sandbox policy is still being formed. It asks that animal welfare legislation be included among the protections that a sandbox cannot modify or disapply. If animal welfare legislation is not excluded, the submission asks that a welfare assessment become a condition of any sandbox involving systems that manage or monitor animals, with the results reported to Defra. The submission also asks that Defra commission the Animal Welfare Committee, an expert committee that advises Defra and the Scottish and Welsh Governments, to advise on AI in animal farming.

The Committee's secretariat confirmed on the same day that the form had arrived. The Committee chooses which policies to review and is under no obligation to report on a proposal, so a visible result may take a long time or may not come at all. By 15 November 2026 the Regulating for Growth Bill had not been introduced in Parliament. UK organisations can co-sign the submission or send the Committee a version of their own, and AIGAH is glad to help with either. Please write to hello@aigah.org.
