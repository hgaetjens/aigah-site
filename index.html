About

Bringing animals into AI governance across the EU and UK.

AI systems increasingly shape the lives of animals – in farming, research, wildlife management, and beyond – yet animals are largely absent from the frameworks now being written to govern AI. AIGAH is a civil-society policy initiative that wants to change that: building the evidence base, engaging policymakers, and connecting the people who can put animals on the AI governance agenda.

Our work

Research

Mapping how AI systems affect animals and where existing and emerging regulation – from the EU AI Act to UK frameworks – leaves them out.

Policy engagement

Translating research into concrete proposals for policymakers, advocates, regulators, and standards bodies in Brussels, London, and beyond.

Field building

Convening researchers, advocates, and technologists so that animal interests are represented wherever AI governance is decided.

Current projects

EU consultation: high-risk AI classification

Live – closes 23 July 2026

The EU AI Office is consulting on draft guidelines for classifying high-risk AI systems under the AI Act. AI failures in animal contexts – such as automated climate control in livestock housing – fall squarely within the risks the guidelines address, yet the draft contains no livestock example. We have prepared a guide for responding within the consultation's scope.

Operational criteria for “non-human welfare”

In progress – expected August 2026

The GPAI Code of Practice names non-human welfare as an example of systemic risk, but offers no definition or assessment method. We are developing operational criteria to close that gap, for circulation ahead of the Code's first review in 2027.

Regulatory gap map: EU and UK

In progress – expected October 2026

A systematic map of where EU and UK regulatory frameworks leave animals out of AI governance – around 30 intervention points, built as a shared resource for advocacy organisations.
