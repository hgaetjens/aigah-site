About AIGAH

Our theory of change

Short version · Working draft, September 2026 · Farmed animals in the EU and UK, to 2030

A theory of change sets out what an organisation is trying to change, and the steps by which its work should get there.

Impact

AI makes conditions for farmed animals visible and open to scrutiny, instead of entrenching factory farming.

Goal for 2030

AI used on farmed animals in the EU and the UK is accountable to people outside the companies that make and use it. General-purpose AI models are tested for harm to animals, welfare data reaches regulators and the public, and breaking the rules has consequences.

AIGAH works in all three layers of AI governance for animals (general-purpose AI models, deployment and enforcement), and a fourth column, the hub, supports all three. Each column starts from our activities and the outputs they produce. Early outcomes are what people then come to know and think, and intermediate outcomes are the changes in behaviour, practice and rules that lead towards the goal for 2030.

GPAI models

What general-purpose AI models must take into account · EU

Activities

We draft criteria and tests for the risks that general-purpose AI models pose to animals, together with advisors and expert reviewers, and we brief the EU AI Office.

Outputs

A document, reviewed by experts, that sets out criteria for harm to animals and tests that AI companies could run on their models.

Early outcomes

Staff at the EU AI Office and at AI companies come to see harm to animals as a risk that can be defined and tested.

Intermediate outcomes

AI companies test their models against concrete criteria for harm to animals, which are part of the GPAI Code of Practice, and they report the results.

Deployment

What farms, slaughterhouses and fish farms must record and disclose · EU and UK

Activities

We document incidents in which automated systems harmed animals, and we respond to consultations. We draft criteria for certification and assurance schemes and prepare the case for the Commission's 2028 review of the AI Act's high-risk list.

Outputs

An evidence bank of cases in which automated systems harmed farmed animals, consultation responses, criteria for certification and assurance schemes, a proposal for a welfare measure comparable across species, and the evidence for adding animal uses of AI to the high-risk list.

Early outcomes

Officials, advisory committees and the people who run certification and assurance schemes come to see AI used on animals as a documented risk, and they have criteria that they can apply.

Intermediate outcomes

EU and UK law, and certification and assurance schemes, require operators who use AI on animals to record welfare data against indicators that are set independently, and to disclose this data.

Enforcement

Using AI to enforce existing law, starting with CCTV in slaughterhouses · UK and Spain

Activities

We draft briefs for policymakers and industry on making AI screening of slaughterhouse CCTV mandatory. We approach the Food Standards Agency, and we look for a partner organisation in Spain.

Outputs

Briefs on AI screening of slaughterhouse CCTV, and on how the legal duty of care applies when AI manages animals.

Early outcomes

Inspectors and lawyers know that evidence flagged by AI exists, and that existing legal duties also apply to animals managed by AI.

Intermediate outcomes

Enforcement bodies and lawyers use AI analysis of slaughterhouse CCTV to enforce existing animal welfare law.

The hub

Supports advocacy organisations in all three layers

Activities

We map where EU and UK rules leave animals out, draft templates and bring together people and organisations working on this.

Outputs

A regulatory gap map, templates, a newsletter and workshops.

Early outcomes

Advocates know where the openings are and have draft text that they can adapt and send.

Intermediate outcomes

Advocacy organisations respond to consultations on AI policy in their own name.

Terms used

GPAI Code of Practice
A code of practice under the EU AI Act for companies that provide general-purpose AI models. It names “non-human welfare” as an example of systemic risk but does not define it. Its first review is expected in 2027.
General-purpose AI models
AI models that can carry out a wide range of tasks, such as the large language models behind chatbots.
EU AI Office
A body within the European Commission that implements the AI Act and oversees the Code of Practice.
High-risk list of the AI Act
Annex III of the EU AI Act lists the areas, such as critical infrastructure and education, in which AI systems carry extra obligations. None of these areas concerns animals. By August 2028 the Commission must report on whether new areas are needed.
Welfare measure comparable across species
A measure of welfare that allows comparison between species, for example time spent in pain of different intensities. It is sometimes called an interspecific welfare metric. Welfare data disclosed by operators could then be read against such a measure.
Certification and assurance schemes
Private standards that farms sign up to, such as Red Tractor, RSPCA Assured and Soil Association in the UK, and the certification schemes used in fish farming.
Duty of care
Under the Animal Welfare Act 2006 (England and Wales), anyone responsible for an animal must take reasonable steps to meet its welfare needs.
Slaughterhouse CCTV
CCTV is mandatory in slaughterhouses in all four UK nations and in Spain. England required it first, in 2018, and Northern Ireland most recently, from August 2026. Spain has required it since 2023. In England, the official vet must view at least 15 minutes of footage a day.
Food Standards Agency
A government department whose official vets oversee animal welfare in slaughterhouses in England and Wales.
